# Three regional lenses

These are study designs to investigate, not claims of violations or measured health effects. Geographic bounding boxes are teaching windows, not jurisdiction boundaries. Adapt the map parameters and record the observation period before interpreting patterns.

## Northern Europe: visibility in busy water

**Window:** longitude −12° to 32°, latitude 48° to 66°. **Question:** how sensitive is unmatched activity to the matching rule in dense shipping areas?

Start with the published SAR activity map. Compare a North Sea window with a Baltic window, keeping color scales and units consistent. Inspect the 2017–2021 coverage layer. Use the satellite catalog exercise to find June 2024 imagery near the southern Baltic, recognizing that it is a different period from the study map.

The [ICEYE article](https://www.iceye.com/blog/dark-vessels-in-northern-europe-and-how-iceyes-satellites-find-them) provides a commercial-SAR perspective. The notebooks use Sentinel-1 research products and synthetic radar pixels. A rigorous follow-up would synchronize image acquisitions with suitable AIS data, account for vessel motion and SAR displacement, and review ambiguous targets manually. Do not compare unsynchronized positions as if they were simultaneous.

**One Health extension:** coastal warming and seafood hazards. Add environmental measurements and public-health surveillance at an appropriate scale. Account for bathing and seafood-consumption patterns before relating environmental conditions to disease reports.

## West Africa: effort, access, and distribution

**Window:** longitude −20° to 0°, latitude 0° to 25°. **Question:** what would additional transparency tell us about pressure, and what would it still leave unknown about local food access?

Map activity and compare matched and unmatched components. Avoid interpreting EEZ-grouped study tables as flag-state behavior. Include artisanal fleets, landing surveys, species composition, market prices, household dietary surveys, and the knowledge of fishing communities in any follow-up study.

**One Health extension:** use the food scenario to hold total catch fixed while varying local retention and losses. If availability changes without a change in total catch, explain why a global catch total is inadequate as a local nutrition indicator. Identify who could gain or lose from each intervention before recommending one.

## Eastern Pacific: pressure is not stock status

**Window:** longitude −95° to −70°, latitude −25° to 5°. **Question:** can a visible activity pattern distinguish a healthy stock from a depleted one?

Use the same observed map and the stock model. Two stocks with different growth rates, recruitment patterns, or initial biomass can respond differently to the same fishing mortality. Apparent fishing hours are even further removed because catchability, gear, species targeting, and technological change affect their relationship to fishing mortality.

**One Health extension:** investigate climate, distribution shifts, and coastal food systems. Match species-specific stock assessments to the relevant fleet and season. Environmental anomalies, trade, management, and access should enter the design separately.

## A shared reporting template

For each region, submit one observed map, one uncertainty plot, one conceptual pathway, and a short evidence ledger. Mark every statement **observed**, **modeled**, **hypothetical**, or **unresolved**. Include the denominator, unit, time range, acquisition or processing version, and the source license. End with a decision the available evidence can support, plus a decision that requires more evidence.
